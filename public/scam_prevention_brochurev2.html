Quick Advice

Top Safety Tips

⚡ The Hesitation Rule

If an offer seems too good to be true or you have even a tiny hint of hesitation: It is a scam!

🚫 The "Refund" Trick

Legitimate organizations will never ask to remote into your screen to refund money or fix an account.

🛑 Take 5 Minutes

Scammers rush and frighten you. Hang up the phone! Talk to a family member, neighbor, or sheriff first.

Keep this guide by your landline or front desk.

Emergency Reference & Community Defense
Rulebook

Anti-Scam Best Practices

1. Assume It's A Scam

Treat unknown callers, unexpected texts, and alarming balance alerts as scams first until proven otherwise with hard proof.

2. Verify Identity

Never give information over the phone—not even your name. Ask who is calling, hang up, and call their verified public number.

3. Call The Sheriff

If someone threatens arrest, warrant service, or immediate fines, hang up immediately and notify the Sheriff's station.

📢 3. Social Scams

Beware of fake roadside "child funeral" fundraisers. Fraud rings stand at busy intersections with donation buckets and laminated photos. Never give cash or click unverified links.

Emergency Action

If You Were Targeted

Do not feel embarrassed—act quickly to lock down your assets:

  1. Inform Law Enforcement FIRST: Contact your local Sheriff or go directly in person to your local police station to get an official police report filed. A formal report number protects your bank claims and disputes.
  2. Disconnect Devices: Immediately turn off your Wi-Fi router or unplug the power cable from your desktop/laptop computer.
  3. Call Your Bank: Dial the fraud department on the back of your card to freeze accounts, halt pending wires, and reissue cards.
  4. File Online Triage: Visit endscams.org/shutdown for an emergency checklist.

🛑 Family Protection Rule:

Scammers demand absolute secrecy. If any caller instructs you not to tell your family or bank tellers, it is 100% a criminal scam.
